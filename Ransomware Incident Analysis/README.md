# Ransomware Incident Analysis – Healthcare Clinic

## Overview

This project documents the analysis of a simulated ransomware security incident affecting a small U.S. healthcare clinic specializing in primary-care services.

The incident began with a targeted phishing campaign in which attackers sent malicious email attachments to employees. After an employee downloaded a malicious attachment, malware was installed on the employee's computer, allowing the attackers to gain access to the organization's network and deploy ransomware.

The ransomware encrypted critical files, including medical records, preventing employees from accessing essential data and software. The incident caused significant disruption to the clinic's operations and resulted in a ransom demand for the decryption key.

This project demonstrates the use of structured incident documentation and the **5 W's framework** to analyze a cybersecurity incident.

## Attack Overview

The simulated attack followed this general sequence:

1. Attackers targeted employees of the healthcare clinic with phishing emails.
2. The phishing emails contained malicious attachments.
3. An employee downloaded the malicious attachment.
4. Malware was installed on the employee's computer.
5. The attackers gained access to the organization's network.
6. Ransomware was deployed.
7. Critical files, including medical records, were encrypted.
8. Employees lost access to essential files and software.
9. A ransom note was displayed demanding payment for a decryption key.
10. The organization shut down its computer systems and sought technical assistance.

## Incident Analysis – The 5 W's

### Who

An organized group of unethical hackers caused the incident. The attackers targeted organizations in the healthcare and transportation industries and used targeted phishing emails to gain initial access to the healthcare clinic's network.

### What

The attackers sent phishing emails containing malicious attachments to several employees. After an employee downloaded a malicious attachment, malware was installed on the employee's computer, allowing the attackers to gain access and deploy ransomware.

The ransomware encrypted critical company files, including medical records, preventing employees from accessing essential data and software. The attackers then displayed a ransom note demanding money in exchange for the decryption key.

### When

The security incident occurred on a Tuesday morning at approximately **9:00 a.m.** Employees began reporting that they were unable to access files and software needed to perform their jobs and that ransom notes were displayed on their computers.

### Where

The incident occurred within the computer network and systems of a small U.S. healthcare clinic specializing in primary-care services.

The attack affected employee computers, critical files, medical records, and other systems required for the clinic's daily operations.

### Why

The attackers gained initial access through targeted phishing emails containing malicious attachments. When the malicious attachment was downloaded, malware was installed on an employee's computer, providing the attackers with a means to gain access to the organization's network and deploy ransomware that encrypted critical files.

## Immediate Response and Remediation

The following actions were identified as appropriate immediate response measures:

* Isolate affected computers from the network to help prevent the ransomware from spreading.
* Initiate incident response and containment procedures.
* Identify potentially affected systems.
* Verify the integrity and availability of backups.
* Determine whether affected systems and data can be safely restored.
* Assess recovery options without relying on payment of the ransom.

---

## Long-Term Prevention

The organization should strengthen its defenses against phishing and malicious attachments through improved email security controls and attachment analysis or sandboxing.

Additional security measures should include:

* Network Access Control (NAC)
* Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP)
* Security awareness training focused on phishing detection
* Reliable offline backups
* Regular backup validation and recovery testing
* Controls designed to reduce the likelihood and impact of ransomware

## Key Takeaway

This incident demonstrates how a targeted phishing email can serve as an initial access vector for a larger ransomware attack.

It also demonstrates the importance of:

* Effective incident documentation
* Rapid containment of affected systems
* Reliable offline backups
* Secure email controls
* Phishing awareness training
* Least-privilege access
* Network access controls
* Structured incident-response procedures

A successful incident response requires organizations to identify the attack, contain its impact, support recovery, and implement controls that reduce the likelihood of similar incidents occurring in the future.
